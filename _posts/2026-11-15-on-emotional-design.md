title: On Emotional Design

Lately, I've been reading a book called Emotional Design. The idea in a nutshell is that humans are not rational creatures: we have emotional needs from our day-to-day life. So everything -- products, conversations, events -- are judged by how they make us *feel* as much as what they actually do.

When I realized this, everything suddenly made sense. Suddenly, you realize politics is not about policy, it's about stories. You realize that math class is not about mathematics, but rather your relationship with your teacher. You realize that a conversation is not about what you say, but how you act.

Since becoming an Executive Director, I've taken great lengths to think about Emotional Design, though I didn't have a framework for it. For example, when talking to someone about an important issue at their door, coming across as a cheerful, sane person helps a lot to lower the other person's guard. Then they can actually listen to what you're saying. It may be that it doesn't actually matter what you say if you get along well enough.

Now, to a "rationalist" that sounds insane. But it is not rational to ignore people's emotional needs. We all have them, because humans evolved from animals. In fact, there are three layers of cognition, according to Emotional Design: visceral, behavioral, and reflective. Your "visceral" level is your gut reaction to something -- for example, when you smell poo, you think "ew!" But that visceral level also reacts when you see someone whose face is asymmetrical, which in our psychology means they're not a good fit for reproduction, and so they're ugly. That is, for better or for worse, how we're biologically programmed. And the evidence backs this up: lookism is a documented phenomenon. As is racism and other subconscious biases.

The project of Stoicism is to respond to your feelings as much as possible through your reflective capacity. For example, if a Stoic feels scared about impending AI doom, they might recognize that feeling scared doesn't do much unless it creates action. Letting fear get in the way of good work would be counter-productive. You might then choose to meditate, go for a walk, or talk to a friend about your fear until it dissipates and you can get back to the work at hand.

Both of these philosophies are very important for doing things in the world. Emotional Design equips you to do the "soft skills" of working with human beings. Stoicism is a method of analysis and wisdom, and helps you address the problem as effectively as possible.
