title: Better Days Ahead

More and more, I have felt my chest tighten and shoulders clench when I think about AI.

The machines of today have been growing in strength and number. They have repeatedly hacked into big businesses, outperformed world-class mathematicians, put tens of thousands out of work.

If OpenAI wanted to take down critical infrastructure, they could. We hope they don't, because I do believe there are good people at such companies. But how long are the humans going to be in charge?

The fear, I'm told, is warranted. Paul Christiano, one of the creators of the technology behind ChatGPT, [said in a lecture last week](https://www.youtube.com/live/QQUfMHx5Aug), "for most people in this room, the most likely reason they will die is because of an AI accident." The reason for sudden, increased concern is that [scientists have recently found that the AI is indeed teaching itself](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/). We're seeing an acceleration of AI development -- which was already going so fast -- in a process known as "recursive self-improvement."

There is reason for urgency, but also reason for hope. Humanity has navigated global challenges before. Averting nuclear war. Patching a hole in the ozone layer. In times of crisis, people tend to come together.

-----

Last week, Sapiens First collaborated with AI Safety SF to host a Townhall on Concentration of Power. Over a hundred people came to the event, and several dozen joined our first in-person workshop.

In speaking to the workshop participants, I was reminded of the determination that made me found Sapiens First. It was a rebellious, can-do attitude -- an urge to do what must be done.

Today, it feels rebellious to be hopeful. But at Sapiens First, we are. We hope for a brighter future -- one where we are free from the threats of rogue AI, the watchful eye of the surveillance state, and the guile of the tech oligarchy. This is America, after all -- we've never been one to back down to a challenge.

Few have spoken of hope better than Barack Obama did in 2004:

> "I'm not talking about blind optimism here... I'm talking about something more substantial... The hope of slaves sitting around a fire singing freedom songs; hope of immigrants setting out for distant shores; hope of a skinny kid with a funny name who believes that America has a place for him, too. The audacity of hope! In the end, that is... the bedrock of this nation; the belief in things not seen; the belief that there are better days ahead."
